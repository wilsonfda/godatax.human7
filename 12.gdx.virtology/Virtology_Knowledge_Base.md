## 1. What Virtology claims to be

Virtology (*Virtologia*) is presented as a "clínico-filosófica" (clinical-philosophical)
school founded by **Eduardo Casarotto**, described on the source site as an independent
researcher. Its central reframing: **"virtue" is redefined away from a moral/ethical concept
and recast as a neurological one** — in the site's own words, *"virtude é um conjunto de redes
neurais trabalhando numa configuração específica"* ("a virtue is a set of neural networks
working in a specific configuration"). Virtues are not character judgments; they are trainable
brain-circuit configurations, built through repetition until they operate automatically,
*"sem esforço, sem vigilância"* ("without effort, without vigilance").

This positions Virtology as a **neuroplasticity-based therapeutic method**: instead of
treating a symptom directly, it targets what it claims are the two upstream neural systems
that generate most non-organic psychological suffering (see §2), on the theory that changing
those systems changes everything downstream of them.

## 2. Theoretical basis — the two primitive systems

Virtology's model rests on two named neural systems, both framed as evolutionarily primitive
and largely unconscious:

### SNO — Sistema Neurofuncional do Orgulho (Neurofunctional Pride System)

Operates through the brain's reward circuit and the regions involved in self-referential
thinking. Claimed manifestations: power-seeking, vanity, need for approval, controlling
behavior, and — described as a paradox worth naming explicitly — **timidity**, framed as shame
about criticism rather than its opposite.

### SNE — Sistema Neurofuncional do Egoísmo (Neurofunctional Selfishness System)

Centers on the prefrontal cortex computing a cost-benefit trade-off between self and others.
The **temporoparietal junction** is described as the normal "brake" on this calculation; in
selfishness, that brake fails to engage. Claimed manifestations: aggression, addiction,
indifference, controlling behavior, and corruption.

Both systems are described as intersecting with, and partly explaining the energetic source
of, Freud's and Anna Freud's defense mechanisms — Virtology's stated position is that it
accepts the defense-mechanism framework but adds an account of *what powers it*.

## 3. The Lei do Fluxo (Law of Flow)

A formula presented as governing how a person perceives and reacts to any given event:

```
PRF = (I × E) × FA × PO × PS × LV
```

| Symbol | Portuguese | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **PRF** | Percepção, Reação, Fluxo | The outcome — how the event is perceived, reacted to, and processed |
| **E** | Estímulo | The stimulus — the event itself |
| **I** | Intensidade | Intensity — the personal weight/significance assigned to the event |
| **FA** | Faixa de Evolução | The person's current evolution level (see §4) |
| **PO** | Propulsores | Drives / underlying needs |
| **PS** | Personalidade | Personality — character structure and defense mechanisms |
| **LV** | Leis da Vida | "Life laws" — no further definition was found on either source page |

No worked numeric example or unit system was found for this formula on either page — it reads
as a conceptual/qualitative model of how the same external event produces different reactions
in different people, rather than a literally computable equation.

## 4. Faixas de Evolução (Evolution Levels)

Described as hierarchical modes of brain function: lower levels prioritize survival, security,
and status; higher levels enable purpose-driven living. The site states **levels cannot be
skipped** — advancement requires actually building the specific neural structures associated
with a level, not just intellectually understanding a higher one.

**Gap:** neither source page yielded an explicit table naming/numbering the individual Faixas
de Evolução (unlike the 33 virtues in §5, which are given as a complete, ordered list). If a
full breakdown of the levels exists, it lives in a manual/PDF not covered by these two pages.

### Structural notes on the list

- **Humildade (#1) is explicitly foundational**: *"A humildade vem primeiro porque sem ela
  nenhuma das outras se sustenta"* ("Humility comes first because without it none of the
  others can be sustained.") — this is the one dependency relationship stated outright.
- **The ordering encodes dependency, not flat categories.** The manual presents the 33 as a
  sequence with each building on earlier ones, rather than as 33 independent, freestanding
  items — closer in spirit to how `5.five.ways.tomas.aquino`'s Five Ways build on each other
  than to `4.kundalini.chakras`'s seven independent, parallel centers.
- **Virtues #15–17** (Tudo Somos Um / Ajuda ao Próximo / Amor ao Próximo) form a
  loosely-identified "upper tier" centered on interconnection and compassion — this is an
  interpretive grouping, not a labeled tier on the source page itself, and should be verified
  before being treated as authoritative.
- On the `/virtologia/` overview page (as opposed to `/manual/`), only 4 of the 33 were named
  directly in running text: **Humildade** (disconnects self-worth from social position),
  **Abnegação** (reweights another person's value in a decision), **Brandura**, and
  **Individuação** — consistent with, and a subset of, the full 33-item list from `/manual/`.

## 6. Clinical / therapeutic framing

- Virtology is positioned as **complementary to medical treatment** for conditions with a
  genetic/organic basis (schizophrenia, autism, bipolar disorder are named explicitly) — not a
  replacement for psychiatric care in those cases.
- For non-organic distress, the claim is that treating the underlying SNO/SNE configuration
  (rather than the symptom) is the more durable intervention.
- **One detailed case study** is presented on the overview page: a female prisoner, described
  as violent and suicidal, unable to receive visitors. After working through exercises in three
  virtues, the source states: *"dois meses depois, o vídeo gravado com ela mostrava outra
  pessoa"* ("two months later, the recorded video of her showed a different person").
- **Testimonials** from licensed psychologists who report having integrated the method into
  their practice are referenced on the overview page (no names or verifiable credentials are
  given).

## 7. Stated relationship to established psychology

The source explicitly positions Virtology relative to three well-known frameworks, rather than
presenting itself as unrelated to prior work:

- **Freud / Anna Freud** — defense mechanisms are accepted as a real and useful framework;
  Virtology's claimed contribution is explaining the neural *power source* behind them (SNO/SNE).
- **Maslow** — the hierarchy of needs is treated as broadly correct, with Virtology's claimed
  addition being an explanation of *why* people get stuck at a given level instead of advancing
  (tied to Faixas de Evolução, §4).
- **Viktor Frankl** — logotherapy's emphasis on purpose/meaning is accepted, with Virtology
  adding the claim that a person needs prior neurological readiness (an adequate Faixa de
  Evolução) before purpose-driven living becomes accessible, rather than purpose being available
  at any stage.

## 9. `GDX_SKILL_LINK_SUGGESTIONS` — this knowledge base, applied to real psychometric data

Despite §1's intro line ("no DDL, mapping, or schema work has been done yet") and the open gap
in §8 item 4 (no *official* virtue → SNO/SNE mapping exists from the source itself), a
project-internal, narrower mapping **does** now exist and is live: a skill in this folder
(`.claude/skills/GDX_SKILL_LINK_SUGGESTIONS/`) that reads GDX_HUMAN's real psychometric
data and suggests, for each of a study's lowest-scoring indicators, which of these 33 virtues
addresses it — not virtue↔SNO/SNE (still an open gap, §8 stands), but **variable↔virtue**, a
project-authored judgment call independent of anything the source site states.

### What it does and doesn't claim

- **Reads real scores, suggests a real virtue, never invents either.** The lowest-scoring
  indicators come from a live query against `7.multidimensional.database`
  (`DW_FACT_PSY_VARIABLE_RESULT` × `DW_DIM_VARIABLE` × `DW_DIM_SEPTENARY_LEVEL`); the suggested
  virtue for each one comes from a fixed judgment call recorded in Firebird
  (`GDX_VT_VARIABLE_VIRTUE_MAP` × `GDX_VT_VIRTUE`, `virtology_model_firebird25.sql`, keyed by
  the variable's `.sav` column) — never a model guess, never an LLM call at report-generation
  time. A variable with no row there is reported as **"no suggestion (review)"**, exactly the
  same "not zero, not guessed, structurally absent" discipline `8.gdx.dashboards`'s heatmaps use
  for an empty cell.
- **Same methodological honesty as the septenary mapping.** Every generated report repeats,
  near the top, the same caveat `SPSS_Septenary_Constitution_Mapping.md` uses: "which virtue
  addresses this deficit" is a qualitative/interpretive correspondence, not a statistical or
  clinical finding.

### 2026-10-18: polarity-aware ranking — `SCALE_POLARITY`

Before this date, both this skill and its mirror (`GDX_SKILL_IDENTIFY_VIRTUES`) assumed every
variable shares the same direction: a low raw score is always the deficit, a high one is always
the strength. That assumption is wrong for a **negative-polarity** variable -- the clearest
example already flagged in `Framework_Associations_*.md` (Section 4) is `EmocNeg` (Negative
Emotions): a *high* score there is not a strength, it is the deficit, and a *low* score is the
already-present strength -- the opposite reading from every other, positive-polarity variable in
the table.

`GDX_VT_VARIABLE_VIRTUE_MAP` now carries a `SCALE_POLARITY` column
(`variable_virtue_map_polarity_firebird25.sql`, `NOT NULL`, default `POSITIVE`), set once per
variable by `GDX_SKILL_ASSOCIATES_VIRTUE_TO_CONSTRUCTOR` when its correspondence is first
recorded -- the same never-invent-the-judgment-call discipline already applied to
`RECORDED_BEHAVIOR` and both justification columns. `EmocNeg`'s existing row was backfilled to
`NEGATIVE`; every other existing row kept the `POSITIVE` default (confirmed correct for all of
them). Both `generate_suggestions.js` and `identify_virtues.js` now fetch every indicator
unordered and rank it by a polarity-adjusted need/strength score (`rankByDeficit()` /
`rankByStrength()`) instead of a plain `ASC`/`DESC` sort on the raw average -- so a
negative-polarity variable's high score is read as its deficit, and its low score as its
strength, automatically. Both reports now print a **Polarity** column per row, and both header
caveats explain the mechanism instead of just warning the reader to check it manually.

A variable with no row in `GDX_VT_VARIABLE_VIRTUE_MAP` yet has no recorded polarity either and
defaults to `POSITIVE` for ranking purposes -- never guessed as `NEGATIVE` from the variable's
name. (`EmocNeg` itself still never appears in either report's ranking for `ID_STUDY=1`, but for
an unrelated, pre-existing reason: its `DW_DIM_VARIABLE.ID_SEPTENARY_LEVEL` is `NULL`, and both
skills' queries inner-join to `DW_DIM_SEPTENARY_LEVEL` -- the same exclusion `Emotional
Exhaustion` hits above, not something this polarity change introduced or was asked to fix.)

## 10. How virtues are suggested from the lowest scores

After a study's data reaches the GoDataX Human 7 data warehouse, every psychometric variable
carries a real average score — computed strictly from that study's own participants, never
blended with another sample's. The first step in suggesting a virtue is simple and deliberately
mechanical: sort those averages from lowest to highest. The reading behind that ordering is
equally simple — the indicator with the lowest score is, within that population, the one
expressing the most acute need at that moment. This is not a diagnosis; it is a statistical
snapshot of where the group stands today, furthest from what the instrument sets out to measure.

The second step is where the system stops being pure statistics and becomes interpretation. For
each of these low-scoring indicators, the framework consults a fixed catalog of correspondences
between psychometric variable and virtue — the 33 trainable virtues described by Eduardo
Casarotto's Virtology, each understood not as a moral judgment but as a configuration of neural
networks that can be built through repetition until it operates without effort. This
correspondence is not calculated, not inferred by an algorithm, and not guessed: it is an
interpretive judgment call, recorded once by a human being who decides, for that specific
variable, which of the 33 virtues addresses the deficit it describes — and why. Once made, that
decision is permanently recorded (who confirmed it, from which institution, at what confidence
level) and holds for any future study that measures the same variable.

A real example illustrates the mechanism. In one of the studies already processed, the
lowest-scoring indicator was "Vigor" (disposition and energy to invest effort) — and the
correspondence recorded for that variable pointed to the virtue **Perseverança** (Perseverance):
the reading is that low vigor is depletion of sustained energy, and Perseverance is exactly the
practice of maintaining effort despite wear — a direct antidote, not a palliative. The second
lowest indicator, "Absorption" (the flow state of losing track of time in an activity), pointed
to **Tudo Somos Um** (We Are All One): the literature describes absorption as a dissolution of
the boundary between subject and object, and that is precisely the existential dimension named
by the virtue of unity/interconnection. Further down the list, "Challenge" — the ability to draw
meaning and growth from adversity — pointed to **Busca do Melhoramento** (Pursuit of
Improvement): here the correspondence is almost a deliberate tautology, because facing
difficulty as an opportunity for growth is, literally, that virtue's own definition.

Each row of this mapping carries two justification sentences, not one — one read through the
lens of deficit ("a low score here calls for this virtue as a treatment target") and a mirrored
one read through the lens of strength ("a high score here already signals that this same virtue
is present"). It is the same conceptual correspondence viewed from both ends: what separates "a
point to work on" from "a resource already present" is not the virtue — it is only which end of
the distribution that particular indicator sits at. A group scoring low on Vigor receives
Perseverança as a suggestion to work on; a group scoring high on that same variable would read
that same Perseverança as a strength already present.

It is essential to mark this tool's limits with the same methodological honesty applied to every
other layer of the framework: the low score is a real number, drawn from the database; the
virtue suggested in response to it is a qualitative, interpretive correspondence, not a
statistical finding, and certainly not a clinical prescription. When a variable has no recorded
correspondence yet, the system never invents one — it appears explicitly as "no suggestion
(review)," an invitation for someone to make that interpretive judgment call deliberately,
rather than for the system to guess it on its own. And there is one more easily-forgotten
caveat: not every variable carries the same sense of polarity — for a negative-pole indicator,
such as "negative emotions," a high score is not a strength at all, it is the opposite; what
counts as deficit or resource always depends on what that specific variable is measuring.
